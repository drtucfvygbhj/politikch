# PolitikCH+ — editorial handbook

How PolitikCH+ articles are planned, written, checked, translated and corrected.
The binding rules are in `GUARDRAILS.md` §15 (ED-01 to ED-13); this handbook is
how to follow them in practice. Adopted by the owner 2026-09-27. Nothing here is
live yet: see `LAUNCH_PLAN.md`.

> Not legal advice. Where this handbook touches the law (right of reply,
> quotation, liability), the open questions are Q15–Q19 in `GUARDRAILS.md` §10.

---

## 1. What we are

**Politikch** is an independent reference for Swiss democracy. Its reference
pages stay neutral: they describe, show the arguments on every side and never
tell anyone how to vote.

**PolitikCH+** is the paid analysis on top. It reaches conclusions. Based on
evidence, an article may criticise or commend a proposal, a party or a campaign,
and may recommend a Yes or a No.

**Non-partisan** (ED-01) means we belong to no party, no editor holds a party
membership, office or campaign role, and we take no money from parties,
campaign committees or organisations campaigning on a pending vote. It does
**not** mean we have no views. It means our views come from the evidence, and
they will favour different sides on different questions.

---

## 2. Article types (ED-03)

| Type | Reaches a conclusion? | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **Explainer** | No | How something works; the background to a vote |
| **Analysis** | Yes: may criticise, commend and recommend a vote | Votes, initiatives, parliamentary decisions, campaigns, party strategy |

A recommendation is always part of an Analysis, never an article of its own.
Both types carry the red border and the **PolitikCH+** label everywhere (ED-02).

---

## 3. The article template

Every Analysis follows this order. An Explainer uses 1, 2, 3 and 7.

1. **Headline and teaser.** Free to read, so they must stand on their own
   evidence (ED-04). No claim in the headline that the body only argues.
2. **The facts.** What happened, with figures. Every figure is sourced. Where
   the data is on the reference site, it is also an internal link (§4.2).
3. **Analysis.** What the evidence shows, step by step.
4. **Both sides** (ED-05). At least one argument for **Yes** and one for **No**
   (or, if the article isn't about a vote, one on each side of its central
   question). You decide per article whether to endorse an argument, test it
   against the facts, or simply present it. **An article without both sides is
   not published.**
5. **Our conclusion.** Your judgement: criticism, praise, and, where you choose,
   a recommendation: *Yes*, *No*, or *No recommendation* (ED-07).
6. **Response** (only when ED-06 applies). The reply of the party or person
   accused, or "did not respond by <date>".
7. **Sources.** Every source, numbered, in full (§4.1).
8. **Corrections.** Any correction, dated (ED-12).

Length: a **brief** is about 800 words; a **deep dive** about 1,800. Choose per
topic; not every proposal needs a deep dive.

## 4. Evidence (ED-04)

### 4.1 Citing sources

- Every figure and every factual claim has a numbered source marker.
- Prefer primary sources: the official record, the parliamentary vote, the
  Federal Council's explanations, the party's own publication. Use news reports
  only for what only they report, and say so.
- The source list at the bottom gives, for each: who published it, title, date,
  link, and the date you checked it.
- Figures must match the reference site's data. If they differ, find out why
  before publishing (POL-02).

### 4.2 Internal data links

When a figure is also shown somewhere on the reference site (a result, a party's
votes in parliament, campaign money, a party recommendation):

- the figure is **underlined and clickable**;
- the link opens that place on the site with the data **highlighted until the
  reader clicks or taps anywhere else**;
- the source list still names the original source, not just our page.

This needs the article renderer and deep-link highlighting (`LAUNCH_PLAN.md`
Stage D). Until then, list the reference page in the source list.

### 4.3 Fact, judgement, inference

Keep them apart in every paragraph (Swiss Press Council guideline 2.3). A
fictional illustration:

| Kind | Example | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Fact | "Party X's MPs voted 40–2 for the bill (final vote, 12 March)." | Sourced |
| Judgement | "That is hard to reconcile with Party X's campaign slogan." | Worded as a judgement and resting on the facts shown |
| Inference about motives | "This suggests the party puts its business supporters first." | Marked as inference ("suggests", "appears"), never stated as fact |
| ❌ Unsupported | "Party X lied to voters." | A serious accusation stated as fact. Needs proof **and** ED-06 |

Words like "hypocritical", "misleading" or "dishonest" are judgements. You may
use them when the facts in the article support them, and they trigger the right
of reply (§5). Insults add nothing and are not used.

## 5. Right of reply (ED-06)

**When:** before publishing a serious accusation against a named party, person
or organisation: misconduct, deception, bad faith, hypocrisy, a broken promise.

**How:**

1. Email the party's press office or the person, from the Politikch address.
2. State the accusation precisely, the facts it rests on, and the deadline:
   **at least two working days**.
3. Put the reply, fairly summarised or quoted, in section 6; or write "did not
   respond by <date>".
4. Keep the email and the reply **outside the public repo** (the Politikch
   mailbox). They are correspondence with named people.

**Template (EN):**

> Subject: Request for comment — PolitikCH+ article on <topic>
>
> Dear <name>, Politikch intends to publish an analysis on <topic> on <date>.
> It will state that <accusation, precisely>, based on <facts, with sources>.
> We would like to include your response. Please reply by <date, time>. If we
> receive no reply, the article will say so. Kind regards, PolitikCH+ editorial

Swiss party press offices usually work in German or French. You may send the
same text in German or French.

---

## 6. Recommendations (ED-07)

- Any vote: federal now, cantonal or communal later.
- The reasoning must engage with both sides (§3.4). "No recommendation" is a
  valid and sometimes the most honest conclusion.
- Publish before the ballot's quiet period (§7).
- Never on a reference page, except as a marked link to the article.
- Not on a vote in which you have a personal interest.
- Wording: "**Our recommendation: No.**", followed by the reason in one or two
  sentences. No imperatives aimed at the reader.

---

## 7. Quiet period (ED-08)

No new Analysis about a proposal on a ballot, and no new serious accusation
about its campaign, in the **10 days before** that ballot. Corrections are
always allowed.

Voters receive their documents at least three weeks before a federal ballot.
Aim to publish **4–6 weeks before**; the last possible day is **day 11 before**.

## 8. AI (ED-09)

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| Research, finding and summarising sources for yourself | Drafting any sentence of an article |
| Outlines, structure, checklists, this handbook | Rewriting or "improving" your sentences |
| Proofreading: marking spelling, grammar, punctuation, consistency and possible factual errors | Accepting a rewording you didn't write |
| Checking figures against sources | Adding text of any length |

**Proofreading rule:** the tool marks; you decide each correction; if a
sentence needs rewording, you reword it.

**What we say publicly:** what is human, positively. "Written by a person."
"Every article is written by our editor." Never "AI-free", "no AI" or similar;
they would be false (we use AI for research and proofreading) and `check.py`
blocks them.

Machine translations are AI output the reader sees, so they are always labelled
(§9).

## 9. Translation (ED-10)

| Language | Version | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **English** | The original, written by the editor; authoritative | — |
| **German** | DeepL, checked by the editor | "Machine translation (DeepL), checked by our editor" |
| **French** | DeepL, checked by the editor | same |
| **Italian** | DeepL, **not checked**. **Not published** when the article has a recommendation or a serious accusation; readers get links to EN/DE/FR instead | "Machine translation (DeepL), not checked by our editor" |
| **Romansh** | None (DeepL has no Romansh) | Readers get links to EN/DE/FR |

- Article text only goes to a **paid** DeepL plan with a data processing
  agreement: the free plan may keep what it receives, and articles aren't public
  (OPS-09).
- Every version links to the English original.
- **Write translation-friendly English:** short sentences, one idea each, the
  subject named, no idioms or wordplay, no ambiguous "it"/"they".
- **Checking German and French:** check every sentence of sections 4–6 against
  the English. There, a mistranslation can turn a judgement into an accusation.
  Read sections 1–3 for sense. Fix errors by adjusting the English or the
  glossary, then re-translate, rather than hand-editing the translation.
- Corrections are made to the original and re-translated (ED-12).

## 10. Byline and anonymity (ED-11)

- Byline: **PolitikCH+ editorial**.
- The About page says truthfully how many people write ("currently one
  editor"), never "our team" while it is one person.
- The editor's name does not appear on the site. The operator (planned: the
  Verein) is named wherever the law requires (Q15, Q19).
- Anonymity hides the editor from readers, not from the law: the author is
  responsible for what they publish. The protection is this handbook.

---

## 11. Corrections (ED-12)

- Confirmed error → corrected within 48 hours.
- A dated note at the end of the article says what was wrong and what changed.
- The correction also goes into the public corrections log.
- Translations are regenerated from the corrected original.
- Readers report errors through the existing corrections contact (POL-11).

---

## 12. Before publishing: checklist

- [ ] Type set (Explainer / Analysis); PolitikCH+ marking will show (ED-02, ED-03)
- [ ] Every figure and factual claim has a source; full source list (ED-04)
- [ ] Figures match the reference site; internal data links point to the right place
- [ ] Headline and teaser stand on their own evidence
- [ ] Facts, judgements and inferences worded as such
- [ ] At least one argument for Yes and one for No (or each side of the question) (ED-05)
- [ ] Serious accusation? → right of reply sent, deadline passed, response included (ED-06)
- [ ] Recommendation? → reasoning covers both sides; no personal interest (ED-07)
- [ ] Not within 10 days of the ballot it concerns (ED-08)
- [ ] Every sentence written by you; AI used only as §8 allows (ED-09)
- [ ] German and French checked, sections 4–6 sentence by sentence; Italian only if allowed (ED-10)
- [ ] Glossary applied; official titles used
- [ ] Waited 24 hours and re-read it (anything critical of a named party or person)
- [ ] Second reader has checked facts against sources (once there is one)

## 13. Calendar and capacity

At 3–4 hours a week (~150–180 hours a year, ~130 for writing):

| Piece | Time | Per year |
|---|---|---|
| Deep dive on the main proposal of each federal ballot | ~12 h | 4 |
| Brief on each other federal proposal | ~5 h | ~8 |
| Post-vote brief | ~4 h | 4 |
| Session review brief (four sessions a year) | ~5 h | 4 |
| **Total** | | **~20 articles** |

Time comes in bursts around ballots. Bank hours between campaigns with pieces
that aren't tied to a date. Before launch, build a backlog of 6–8 articles
(`LAUNCH_PLAN.md` Stage D).
